Retirement Accounts & Pensions - Tax Implications
401(k) Division (QDRO Required)
Tax Implications:
• Direct QDRO transfer = NO immediate tax
• Receiving spouse: No tax until withdrawal
• Rollovers to IRA available (defer taxation)
• Early withdrawal (before 59½) = 10% penalty + income tax (unless QDRO exception)
• Requires Qualified Domestic Relations Order (QDRO)

Recommendation: Use QDRO to avoid immediate taxation. Receiving spouse can roll over to Traditional or Roth IRA for continued tax deferral.
IRA Division (Spousal Rollover)
Tax Implications:
• Direct transfer (not rollover) = NO immediate tax
• Receiving spouse: Treats as own IRA
• No RMDs until reaching age 73 (SECURE 2.0)
• Early withdrawal (before 59½) = 10% penalty + income tax
• Exception: Up to [amount] can be rolled to Roth (penalty-free)

Recommendation: Use trustee-to-trustee transfer to avoid withholding. Consider Roth conversion for tax planning.
Pension Division (QDRO)
Tax Implications:
• QDRO creates separate payee for alternate payee
• Distributions taxed to receiving spouse only
• No early withdrawal penalty with QDRO
• Lump sum options may have different tax treatments
• Survivor benefits must be addressed

Recommendation: Determine lump sum value (QDRO will require actuary). Consider present value vs. future payment stream. Survivor benefits critical for younger spouses.

Real Estate - Tax Implications
Primary Residence Transfer
Tax Implications:
• No immediate capital gains tax on transfer (§1041 exchange)
• Receiving spouse receives stepped-up basis for appreciation
• If sold later: Only gain AFTER divorce taxed (at preferential rates if <[amount] single / [amount] joint)
• Mortgage interest & property tax deductions continue
• Transfer cannot trigger "due on sale" clause (divorce exception)

Recommendation: Primary residence transfers are VERY tax-efficient. Ensure title transfer is done correctly to avoid issues. Document stepped-up basis calculation.
Rental Property / Investment Real Estate
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No tax on transfer in divorce
• Receiving spouse takes over depreciation recapture liability
• Depreciation claimed will be recaptured (25% rate) on future sale
• Basis adjustments important for both parties
• Mortgage interest deductions transfer with property

Recommendation: Calculate depreciation recapture liability for balance sheet. Consider who should receive property based on future tax implications. Update Form 4797 records.
Vacation Home / Second Home
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No transfer tax in divorce
• If used as rental: Depreciation recapture applies
• If used personally: Capital gains treatment on appreciation
• Mortgage interest only deductible if primary residence or one other home
• State transfer taxes may apply (state law dependent)

Recommendation: Clarify intended use after divorce. Consider mortgage interest deduction implications if receiving spouse doesn't have another deductible home.

Investment Accounts - Tax Implications
Brokerage Account Transfer
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No recognition of gain/loss on transfer
• Basis carries over (no stepped-up basis)
• Receiving spouse inherits capital gain liabilities
• Unrealized gains will be taxed when receiving spouse sells
• Dividend/interest income transfers to receiving spouse

Recommendation: Calculate unrealized gain liabilities for each security. Consider tax-loss harvesting opportunities. Document cost basis for future sales.
Mutual Fund Distribution During Divorce
Tax Implications:
• Distributions during divorce year taxed to original owner
• After transfer: Distributions taxed to receiving spouse
• Reinvested dividends create new basis lots
• Capital gains distributions = ordinary income
• Careful timing of transfer important for tax efficiency

Recommendation: Time transfer to avoid mid-year distributions. Track dividend reinvestment for basis calculations. Consider market conditions timing.
Stock Options & Restricted Stock Units (RSUs)
Tax Implications:
• Complex valuation issues (vesting vs. unvested)
• Unvested options: Typically $0 value in division
• Vested options: Valued at fair market value
• RSUs: Taxed as ordinary income when vested
• QDRO may be required for plan division
• Timing of exercise/vesting affects taxation

Recommendation: Obtain valuation from company. Use QDRO if applicable. Consider tax implications of exercise timing. May want offsetting compensation.

Business Interests - Tax Implications
S-Corp / C-Corp Stock Division
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No gain/loss on transfer
• Basis carries over to receiving spouse
• S-Corp: Receiving spouse becomes shareholder (K-1 reporting)
• C-Corp: Dividends taxed at corporate + individual level
• Stock redemption: May trigger tax on corporation/spouse
• Consider operating agreement restrictions

Recommendation: Obtain professional business valuation. Review operating agreement for buyout provisions. Consider recapture taxation.
Partnership Interest Division
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: Transfer not taxable event
• Receiving spouse becomes partner (K-1 reporting)
• Allocations of income/loss transfer with interest
• Liabilities of partnership transfer (at-risk limitation)
• Unrealized receivables: Ordinary income treatment
• Goodwill: Capital gain treatment

Recommendation: Obtain K-1 history. Calculate unrealized receivables separately. Consider cash flow impact on both parties. May require partnership agreement amendment.
LLC / Sole Proprietorship Transfer
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No immediate tax on transfer
• Operating assets: Basis carries over
• Goodwill: May be valued separately
• Schedule C income/expenses transfer with business
• Self-employment tax implications
• Working capital adjustments needed

Recommendation: Professional business valuation essential. Include tangible assets, inventory, customer lists, goodwill. Consider who can operate business. May need operating agreement amendment.

Other Assets - Tax Implications
Life Insurance Cash Value
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No tax on transfer in divorce
• Receiving spouse takes over tax basis
• Surrender charges (company policy dependent)
• Death benefits receive income tax exemption
• Basis increases with premiums paid
• Cash value subject to creditor claims

Recommendation: Obtain policy illustration showing current cash value, death benefit, and surrender costs. Consider who maintains policy. Insurance trust planning may be needed.
Collectibles & Personal Property
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No tax on transfer
• Basis carries over for future sales
• Collectibles taxed at 28% rate (vs. 15-20% for investments)
• Valuation may be challenging (professional appraisal needed)
• Depreciation: Not allowed on personal use property
• Art/Antiques: Professional valuation recommended

Recommendation: Professional appraisals for valuable items. Keep documentation for basis. Consider collectible taxation rates (28%) when dividing. Condition/authenticity critical.
Accounts Receivable / Contingent Assets
Tax Implications:
• §1041 applies: No transfer tax
• Receivables taxed as ordinary income when collected
• Contingent assets: Valued at settlement date
• Lawsuit settlements: May have tax consequences
• Bonus/deferred compensation: Income tax to recipient
• Goodwill from business transition

Recommendation: Carefully value contingent assets. Consider timing of receipt. Tax treatment of various proceeds types. May need escrow for uncertain amounts.
