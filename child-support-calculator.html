State Guideline Information
Income Shares Model (Most Common)
How it works:
1. Calculate combined parental income
2. Determine each parent's percentage share
3. Apply guideline percentage to combined income
4. Each parent pays their share based on income %
5. Adjust for childcare, health insurance, parenting time

Example: Combined income [monthly amount], guideline 20% = [support amount]

State-Specific Variations
California: Guideline percentage increases with more children (20% → 25% → 30% → 35%)
Texas: Cap on guideline calculations (~[amount] combined income) with percentage-based model
Florida: Percentage varies; mandatory review if 3+ years or substantial change
New York: Guideline applied to combined parental income; can deviate for shared custody
Most States: Similar income shares model with state-specific adjustments

Common Adjustments & Deviations
Mandatory Adjustments (Included in Calculation)
✓ Childcare: Work-related daycare expenses (added to guideline)
✓ Health Insurance: Cost of child's health/dental insurance (added to guideline)
✓ Parenting Time Adjustments: Custody arrangement and overnights
✓ Income Calculation: Varies by state (gross wages, self-employment, bonuses, etc.)
Discretionary Deviations (Court Can Order)
• Financial Resources: Standard of living, assets, debts
• Earning Ability: Age, health, education, job market
• Substantial Shared Custody: Reduction if parenting time is equal/near-equal
• Other Support Obligations: Spousal support, prior children support
• Tax Benefits: Dependent exemptions, child tax credit
• Special Needs: Medical, educational, mental health costs
• Independent Income of Child: If child has own earnings
• Educational Expenses: College education goals
Important: Guideline Presumed Correct Unless Unjust
Most state laws presume the guideline amount is correct and appropriate. Courts can only deviate if they make specific findings that application would be "unjust and inappropriate." Deviation must be documented in writing with specific reasoning.

Additional Resources & Information
Court Modifications
• Substantial Change: Support can be modified if circumstances change substantially (usually 10%+ income change)
• Periodic Review: Most states allow review every 3 years or upon request
• Emancipation: Support terminates when child reaches age of majority (18-21 depending on state)
• College Support: Some states extend obligation through college if parent is able
Related Considerations
• Tax Filing Status: Dependent exemption / child tax credit allocation
• Medical Insurance: Who carries coverage & pays premiums
• Parenting Plan: Custody type affects support calculation significantly
• College Expenses: Some states have provisions for post-secondary education
• Spousal Support: May affect income available for child support
⚠️ Consult an Attorney
This calculator provides general estimates based on common guidelines. Child support laws vary significantly by state and depend on specific circumstances. Always consult with a family law attorney licensed in your state to ensure accuracy and compliance with your specific situation and state law.
