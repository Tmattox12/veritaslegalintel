Hug & Nelson RSU Analysis

Arizona-compliant equity allocation methodology for marital and separate property RSU awards

Arizona Case Law: Equity Allocation Framework

Hug v. Hug — Establishes the time-based allocation method for equitable distribution of unvested equity awards. The marital portion is determined by the ratio of service time during the marriage to total service time from grant to vesting.

Nelson v. Nelson — Clarifies application of allocation formulas to awards with post-separation vesting. Emphasizes the distinction between service-based and performance-based vesting schedules.

Citations: A.R.S. § 25-211 (community property); § 25-318 (marital property); § 25-211(B)(11) (personal injury awards); § 25-204 (marital vs. separate property)

Allocation Methodologies
Hug (Time-Based) Formula
Allocates the marital portion based on the ratio of service time during the marriage to total service time from grant to vesting.
Marital % = (Separation Date - Grant Date) / (Vest Date - Grant Date)
Nelson (Separation-Aware) Formula
Accounts for post-separation vesting by measuring marital service up to separation against total employment tenure from hire to vest.
Marital % = (Separation Date - Grant Date) / (Vest Date - Hire Date)
Selection Guidance: Choose Hug when the marriage encompasses the grant date (most common). Choose Nelson when service predates the grant or when post-separation vesting must be carefully measured. Consult case law and local precedent; methodology selection materially affects outcome.

Legal Analysis & Guidance
Important: This analysis applies Arizona Hug and Nelson methodologies for educational purposes. The correct allocation method depends on:
  • Whether the marriage encompasses the grant date
  • The nature of vesting schedules (time-based vs. performance-based)
  • Employer treatment of post-separation awards
  • Current Arizona court precedent in your jurisdiction

Recommendation: Obtain legal review from Arizona family law counsel before relying on this analysis. Case law is frequently updated, and fact patterns affect methodology selection significantly.
